Privacy

The short version: your steps and your pup stay on your iPhone.

Last updated: 23 November 2026. Soodle is made by Chloe Morgan.

What Soodle uses

With your permission, Soodle reads your step count from Apple Health. It uses your steps only to show your progress, your pup's mood, hearts and streaks. Your steps, and everything else Soodle saves (your pup, hearts, streaks and settings), stay on your iPhone. Your steps, your name and your Health data are never sent to us or anyone else, and nothing is used for advertising.

Anonymous usage counts

Soodle sends a few anonymous counts to our own database, with no analytics company involved. They tell us how many people open the app and which parts they use: the first time Soodle is opened, opening it each day, opening the shop or decorating, buying something in the shop (which item and how many hearts) and opening a reward. Each count carries a random code made by the app (it is not your name, Apple ID or advertising ID, and a new one is made if you reinstall), your iOS version, the Soodle version and your language setting. Our database host may see your internet address while receiving a count, but we don't save it. Your steps, name and Health data are never included, nothing is used for advertising or shared with advertisers, and the counts can't be linked back to you. In the European Economic Area and Switzerland, Soodle asks you first and sends nothing until you say yes. Everywhere else the counts are on by default. You can switch them off, or change your answer, any time in Soodle's Settings under "Anonymous usage counts".

How long it's kept and deleting it

Soodle keeps this on your iPhone until you delete the app. Deleting Soodle from your iPhone deletes everything it saved. To stop Soodle reading your steps, open the Health app, tap your picture, then Privacy › Apps, and choose Soodle.

This website

soodle.co.uk doesn't use cookies or load anything from other companies' servers, and it uses the fonts already on your device. The only thing counted is a tap on one of our links to the App Store (for example from TikTok or Instagram): we record that the link was tapped and which one, in our own database, with no cookies and nothing that identifies you.

Lucky Scoops

The Lucky Scoops game saves your progress only in your own browser, on your device. It also sends a few anonymous counts to our own database: that the game was opened, which level you are on and each order you finish (with its star rating). They carry a random code made on your device, with no name, email or account, so we can see roughly how far players get. Clearing your browser data starts a new anonymous code.
